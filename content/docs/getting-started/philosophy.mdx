I started programming because I genuinely enjoyed it. Somewhere along the way, the enjoyment got buried under outcomes, comparisons, tools, and expectations.

The Craft is the attempt to dig it back out.

## What that means in practice

- **Fundamentals over fashion.** Frameworks and stacks rotate every few years; operating systems, algorithms, and networks have outlived all of them. Time invested in fundamentals compounds.
- **Process over outcomes.** The point of a session is the session. Finishing something impressive is welcome; showing up is the requirement.
- **Understanding over accumulation.** A topic counts as explored when it can be explained, used, and broken on purpose — not when it has been scrolled past.
- **Honesty over optics.** This documentation records what actually happened, including confusion, dead ends, and abandoned ideas. Nothing is written to look busy.

## Why "the craft"

Software is a craft in the old sense: a practice you get better at slowly, with your hands, by making things and studying how others made them. Mastery is not a milestone; it is a direction.

That is also why the project is not restricted to 90 days. The 90 days rebuild the habit. The craft is the lifetime part.
