## The commitment

One session per day, roughly an hour, currently in the morning. The first 90 days are a commitment to rebuild the habit of showing up; the practice itself is meant to continue well beyond that.

A session can be:

- Studying a concept and working through examples.
- Writing, reading, or debugging code in `labs/`.
- Running a small experiment to test an assumption.
- Designing something, or investigating why something failed.
- Solving a problem from a book, a CTF, or daily work.

There is no requirement to produce something impressive. The objective is to show up and practice.

## Recording a session

Every session gets a short Markdown file under `sessions/<year>/` in the repository, named `YYYY-MM-DD-<short-topic>.md`, created from [the session template](/sessions/templates/daily-session).

Session notes are also rendered read-only on this site under [`/sessions`](/sessions) — listed by year, exactly as they were written.

The template is intentionally light:

- Objective
- Activities
- What I learned
- Experiments / Implementation
- Problems encountered
- References
- Outcome
- Next exploration

Bullet points are fine. Three honest lines are worth more than a polished report that cost the whole session.

## From session to documentation

Sessions are raw material. If a session produces something worth keeping — an explanation that finally clicked, a working experiment, a decision with real trade-offs — the durable part is distilled into the relevant section of this documentation, and linked from the session note.

The `sessions/` directory answers *what did I do on that day*; the documentation answers *what do I actually know now*. Both are served on this site — raw records under [`/sessions`](/sessions), distilled knowledge under `/docs`.
