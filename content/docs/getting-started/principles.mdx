---
title: Working Principles
description: The rules that keep this knowledge base honest and useful.
---

These principles govern how content is produced and maintained across The Craft.

## 1. Only record what actually happened

No placeholder articles, no summaries of things not yet studied. An empty folder means "reserved, not yet explored" — and that is fine.

## 2. Write for the future reader, who is me

Every page should be understandable months later, without the context of the session that produced it. Explain the why, not just the what.

## 3. Prefer primary sources

When something is learned from a book, a specification, or official documentation, the note references the source. Secondhand summaries are a starting point, never the destination.

## 4. Verify by doing

Wherever practical, a concept is backed by code in `labs/` — a small program, a benchmark, a broken-and-fixed example. Reading alone does not close the loop.

## 5. Distill, do not duplicate

Knowledge lands in exactly one section. When two sections touch the same subject, they cross-link instead of copying — for example, operating systems theory lives in [Computer Science](/docs/computer-science), while hands-on Linux work lives in [Systems & Infrastructure](/docs/systems-and-infrastructure).

## 6. Revise instead of abandoning

Understanding changes. When a page turns out to be wrong or incomplete, it gets updated rather than left to mislead.

## 7. Structure serves the work

The repository layout is a scaffold, not a cage. New sections, folders, or lab directories appear when the work justifies them — not to fill a diagram.
